## 07:20-08:40 Warm-up redesign (offline, no robot motion)

**Question** (user): does it make sense to move more than 2-4 cm in the warm-up?

**Method** (`fabric_run.sh warmup-design`, scripts/hw/warmup_design.py; results in
`sessions/2026-11-21_warmup_design`). Each candidate motion is simulated from the real warm-up start corners
(warmup_10_0.1_111137): the robots follow the profile, the hands stay still or follow the person cues. A "truth"
sheet (the cell twin, measured material) is sampled as depth points; the calibration's forward model is run with
stretch/shear/bend moved -1, -0.5, +0.5, +1 decade and each of 8 grip offsets moved 1 cm. Reported: the RMS change
of the depth residual, the part of it no grip-offset error can imitate ("unique", least squares over the 8 offsets),
whether that exceeds the calibration's detection limit (6.4 mm: with ~20 mm model error left, the objective must rise
by 1 mm), and Cramér-Rao sd (for comparing candidates only: white noise assumed).

**The tool reproduces the hardware.** For the 21 s warm-up it predicts shear +1 decade 27.9 mm (hardware scan on
2026-11-20: +9.6 mm objective, i.e. ~25 mm signal), shear -1 decade 4 mm (hardware: not detected), bending < 3 mm
(hardware: flat). So it is used to choose the motion before running it.

**Conclusions**
- Yes, more motion helps, but for the grip points and the bounds, not for detecting a softer shear or bending. The
  deployed motion cuts the grip-point uncertainty from 0.48 to 0.12 cm (4x), the bending bound 10x (1.29 -> 0.13
  decades) and the shear bound 2.8x (0.196 -> 0.071). The grip points are what the warm-up actually calibrated on
  2026-11-20 (factor G: 27.3 -> 19.5 mm), so this is the useful gain.
- A one-decade softer shear or any bending change stays below the detection limit in every quasi-static warm-up
  (unique 3-5 mm per frame). Each frame's signal does not grow with longer motion; only the accumulated (Cramér-Rao)
  precision does, and the real model error (~20 mm, correlated) does not average out like white noise.
- The robots cannot move more than ~10 cm: the validated workspace boxes (bridge.yaml, 2026-11-19 sweep) are
  +/-12 cm around the grasp, and the deployed excursions keep >= 2.7 cm to the faces. The person can move the free
  edge further, hence the spoken hand cues.
- The recorded carry is ~3x more informative per frame (grip offset 1 cm -> 10 mm vs 4 mm). There the hands moved
  45-70 cm (hand edge +/-25 cm relative to the robots, hands within 12 cm of each other) at up to 30-40 cm/s
  (motion_stats.txt).
- Size, not speed: 25 cm hand moves bring a softer shear closest to detectable (5.2 mm) and give the best bounds;
  the same moves at 30 cm/s add nothing over slow ones. So the deployed warm-up uses the larger, slow hand moves.
  Larger still is what the carry did, but needs the person far from a repeatable pose.
